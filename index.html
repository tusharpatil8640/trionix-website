Thin, hot panels fail first

Panels made from thin sheet flex, rust at the welds and let the door sag until the gasket no longer seals. Inside, drives and power supplies heat the air with no planned way out, and hot electronics age fast and trip without warning.

IP protection

Dust and moisture cause the trips nobody can explain

Fine dust settles on contacts, moisture condenses inside on cold nights, and a wash-down jet finds the one gland that was never sealed. The result is corrosion, earth faults and nuisance trips that come and go with the weather.

We choose the IP rating for where the panel will live, from a clean control room to a wash-down area, then make every gasket, louvre, fan and cable gland hold that rating. For humid sites we add a thermostat-controlled space heater, so moisture does not condense inside.

Analog, digital and power wiring run in separate ducts. Signal cables are shielded and earthed at one point, and signal isolators break earth loops where field devices need them. Each field signal lands on its own labelled terminal group.

Layer 1 · Hardwired I/O

A failed transmitter should never look like a good reading

When a transmitter fails or a wire breaks, a basic PLC program may keep showing the last value, or a value that looks normal. The plant runs on a wrong number until someone walks to the field to check.

Trionix LoopSenseNAMUR NE43 + HART diagnostics

We use the live zero of 4–20 mA with NAMUR NE43 fault levels: below 3.6 mA or above 21 mA is treated as a failed device, not a process value, and the loop goes to a safe state with an alarm. With HART on the same pair, we read device diagnostics and configure instruments from the control room.

Layer 2 · Fieldbus

Random communication faults are rarely random

Drives, meters and analysers on Modbus or Profibus drop off the network now and then. The usual cause is a missing termination resistor, wrong biasing, a star-wired segment or cable laid next to power, yet the devices get the blame.

Trionix BusCheckRS-485 done right

We lay out each RS-485 segment as a daisy chain with termination and biasing at the right ends, shielded twisted-pair cable away from power, and every address on a drawing. The PLC then reads current, speed, energy and faults from every device and writes setpoints back, over one cable instead of dozens of wires.

Layer 3 · Industrial Ethernet

One cut cable should not stop the whole line

Many plants run on a flat, office-style network with unmanaged switches. One damaged cable, or one faulty device flooding the network, can stop PLCs, HMIs and drives at the same time.

Trionix RingNetMRP ring + managed switches

PLCs, HMIs, SCADA and remote I/O are connected in a ring using the Media Redundancy Protocol (MRP). If one cable is cut, the network reroutes within 200 ms. Managed switches separate the traffic, and fibre is used for long or noisy runs.

Inside the bi-drum

Why drums trip, prime and scale, and how we stop it

When steam demand jumps, drum pressure falls and the water “swells”. A simple level loop reads this wrongly, closes the feed valve, and a minute later the boiler trips on low water. High TDS makes the water foam and carry over into the superheater, where deposits overheat and crack the tubes. Manual blowdown is either too much, wasting hot water and fuel, or too little, leaving scale.

Trionix DrumGuardThree-element level + auto TDS

Steam flow and feed-water flow act as feed-forward to the level loop, so the drum holds steady through shrink and swell. An automatic TDS loop opens continuous blowdown only as far as conductivity demands. Live trends warn you of carry-over before it reaches the superheater, and low-water alarms go straight to WhatsApp.
